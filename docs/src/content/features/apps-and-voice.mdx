The launcher replaces the home screen, so it also launches the other installed apps. It does so through a dock and a full-screen app panel, both sized for touch accuracy at automotive distances.

## Dock

An application dock holds seven buttons: **Phone**, **Apps**, **Music**, **Navigation**, **Browser**, **Assistant** and **Settings**. **Phone** opens the device's phone app at its dial pad, or the contacts app on a device that has no phone app. **Music** and **Browser** open the device's default music app and browser. The sections below describe the other buttons.

**Settings → Screen → Dock position** attaches the dock to the bottom, top, left or right edge. At the bottom or top, **Dock width** chooses a compact or an extended bar; at the left or right, the dock is a rail of fixed width. A long-press edits the dock: buttons and status icons reorder or hide individually. **Reset dock**, in the edit menu or under **Settings → Screen**, restores the default layout.

## Destinations

The dock's navigation button opens a full-screen destination panel. Type or speak a place or an address, then tap **Open in maps app**: the launcher hands the destination to whichever navigation app the device uses, with no provider built in. A saved place starts navigation to a frequent destination with one tap. **Save current location** adds the current position as a saved place. The pencil on a saved place gives it a name of your own, which the row shows above the address, coordinates or text it was saved from and which a saved point carries to the maps app; clearing the name puts the row back as it was. Typing, saving, renaming and deleting pause while the Global Positioning System (GPS) reads the vehicle as moving; voice and a tap on a saved place work at any time. The panel's header link opens the maps app at the current position. When no app on the device can open a destination, the panel says so under the search field and asks you to install a maps or navigation app; until then, **Open in maps app** and a tap on a saved place hand off nothing. Nightly builds add a **Show route** action that draws the route on the launcher's own map instead, and a long press on that map opens the panel at the pressed point, with **Open in maps app** and **Show route** for it; [Live map](/features/live-map/) describes both.

Another app can hand the launcher a destination too. Share a place from a maps app, or open a map link, and pick **Set as destination**: the destination panel opens with the place filled in, a position as a point ready for **Open in maps app** or **Save point** (and **Show route** in nightly builds), and a name or an address in the search field. Nothing starts by itself, and the panel opens while the vehicle moves as well, since the choice was made in the other app. The launcher never opens a shared link, so a link that carries no position and no place name gets a short message instead. Because the launcher also takes map links, Android asks which app should open a map link until you pick one with **Always**, also on a device where a single maps app used to open such links directly.

## App panel

A full-screen application panel offers search, a pinned-apps row, recently used apps and an A–Z fast-scroll index. The **Display options** menu, behind the panel's ⋮ button, switches between a grid and a list layout, sets one of five icon sizes, from **Extra small** to **Extra large**, and turns **Show hidden apps** on or off.

A long-press on an app opens its menu: **Pin** or **Unpin** adds the app to the pinned row or removes it, **Hide** or **Unhide** takes the app out of the app list and the recent apps or brings it back, **App info** opens Android's page for the app, and **Uninstall** removes an app that is not a system app. A hidden app stays in the pinned row if it is pinned. A long-press on a pinned app enters an edit mode where pins reorder by dragging or leave the row; **Done** ends it.

## Voice assistant

The dock's **Assistant** button, a microphone, starts voice input. **Settings → Screen → Assistant** chooses what the button opens:

- **System**, the default, hands off to the device's own assistant, which opens over the dashboard.
- **Built-in** opens the launcher's voice sheet, which captures speech with a live transcript and hands the recognized text to the device's web search. The sheet falls back to the system voice assistant when no on-device recognizer exists or when you deny the microphone permission: its rows for the system assistant open the device's **Assistant**, **Voice command** or **Voice search**.

The voice sheet also opens with **System** when the device has no assistant. The launcher brings no assistant of its own: recognition runs through the device's own speech service, and the request goes to the assistant the device already has.
