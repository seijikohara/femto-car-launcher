The dashboard background is a live map that fills the whole display and follows the vehicle. The cards, the clock, the speed readout and the dock float above the map.

## Two map providers

- **OpenStreetMap (default).** OpenStreetMap data rendered through the keyless OpenFreeMap service. The provider needs no account and no application programming interface (API) key.
- **Google Maps (optional).** Roadmap, satellite, hybrid and terrain map types with a traffic overlay. The provider activates when you enter a personal Google Maps Platform API key under **Settings → Map**. Usage bills your own Google Cloud account; Femto Car Launcher adds no fees. The app stores the key in its settings and sends it only to Google. The Google Maps provider is not offered in the territories on Google's [Prohibited Territories](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-prohibited-territories) list; the default provider is unaffected. The [Terms of Service](/terms/) give the details that apply to each provider.

## Bring your own style

The OpenStreetMap provider can load a hosted MapLibre style you name (**Settings → Appearance → Map color → Custom style URL**), including a style whose provider key is part of the URL. The map then shows that style's own credits. If you only want a mirror of the default provider's layout (a tile server closer to you, or one you run yourself), set **Settings → Map → Tile host (advanced)** instead.

## A car-navigation camera

- The camera follows the Global Positioning System (GPS) position with easing and keeps the direction of travel pointing up. A tap on the compass toggles a north-up mode.
- A drag detaches the camera for free panning. The camera re-attaches after a pause, or immediately when you tap the locate button.
- Zoom buttons and a compass sit at the screen edge, within reach of the driver on either side of the car.

## Route preview (nightly builds)

Nightly builds can draw a route on the OpenStreetMap provider. Tap **Show route** in the destination panel, under **Open in maps app** or on a saved place, and the launcher computes the route on the device from the current GPS position with the BRouter engine. An old position does not count as where you are: the launcher then asks the device for its current position, which can take up to about half a minute, and the panel says what is missing if none comes or while location access is off. A long press on the map opens the destination panel at the pressed point, with **Open in maps app** and **Show route** for that point; the launcher keeps the point only if you tap **Save point**. Two chips under **Show route**, **Avoid motorways** and **Avoid tolls**, set the roads a route avoids; the launcher remembers them for later routes and keeps a route's choice when it reroutes. Avoiding motorways makes a route prefer other roads and take a motorway only where no reasonable way round exists, while avoiding tolls never uses a toll road, so a destination only a toll road reaches gets no route and the panel says so. The routing data comes from brouter.de, built from OpenStreetMap, one 5° × 5° area at a time: the first route through an area shows the download size and downloads only after you confirm, and the data then stays on the device for later routes. brouter.de rebuilds the data every week, so before a route is computed, the launcher asks brouter.de whether it has rebuilt each area the route uses that it last asked about a week ago or longer. Nothing downloads for an area that has not changed. Newer data never holds a route back: the panel shows its download size, **Update** downloads it, and **Use stored data** computes the route at once with the data on the device and asks again a week later. When the route also needs an area that is not on the device yet, one confirmation covers both downloads, since the route cannot do without the missing area, and **Cancel** stops that route. The launcher checks only when you route, never in the background. **Settings → Map → Routing data** lists the areas on the device with their size and the date of their last check, and deletes one area or all of them after asking. Text you route to goes to the device's geocoder to find the place, so routing to text needs a device with one; a saved position routes without it. The map draws the line, and a row under the speed readout shows the distance left, the arrival time and a button that clears the route. When you drive off the route for a few seconds, the launcher routes again on its own from where you are to the same destination: the row says **Rerouting…**, and the new line replaces the old one. Rerouting uses only the routing data already on the device and never goes to the network, so where an area is missing, the old line stays and the row says there is no routing data. The arrival time knows no traffic, and the launcher gives no turn-by-turn guidance: **Open in maps app** still hands the destination to your navigation app. A long route can take a minute to compute or run out of time; a closer destination works better. The route lasts until you clear it, and it comes back when the app restarts unless the vehicle was parked, since the route was last driven, for longer than **Settings → Location → Start a new trip automatically** allows (2 hours by default); with **Never**, it comes back until you clear it. For that, the launcher keeps the destination, the roads to avoid, the line of the route, with its length and travel time, and the time the route was last driven in files on the device, outside backup, and clearing the route deletes them. That time comes from the latest GPS fix the dashboard received while on screen with the route on. Unless the setting is **Never**, when the device's clock reads more than five minutes earlier than that time, as on a unit that boots before its clock is set, the route stays hidden until the first GPS fix tells how long the vehicle was parked. That fix also removes a route that came back on the device's clock when it shows a longer park, and drops a hidden one when it cannot tell either. A route that comes back returns alone, without other routes to choose from, and its first reroute searches from scratch, so it can take longer. Route preview stays in nightly builds until I have checked it on a head unit.

Once the first route is found, the launcher looks for up to two more routes to the same destination under the same conditions, one after the other, and a small card over the map shows each route as it is found, side by side with its travel time and length. The map draws the route found first, and the speed readout counts it down; tap another route on the card to follow it instead. While the card is open, the routes you have not chosen show as thin grey lines under the chosen one. A route along nearly the same roads as one already listed is left out, and when no other route turns up, the card says so. **Done** closes the card and keeps the route you chose, and the card also closes by itself after 30 seconds of driving. Driving off the route closes the card too, since the other routes started from where you were.

## Depth and style

Optional three-dimensional buildings and terrain relief add depth on the OpenStreetMap provider. Map colors default to the Standard scheme, a clean palette for both the light and the dark theme. They can instead follow the launcher's accent color, or use the provider's own styles unchanged.

## Automatic recovery

When the map fails to load (an unstable link, a provider outage, a missing key), a notice appears inside the visible map area instead of a blank panel. While the launcher is on screen, reloads retry with a capped backoff until the map data loads, even while the device still reports a connection. A reconnect after an offline period reloads the map at once. Behind another app the map waits: if a reload was due, the reload runs once when you return to the launcher. A request the provider answers with a refusal, such as a style address its server reports as missing or a rejected key, stops the retries after a few attempts. A failure that brings back no readable answer, such as a server name that does not resolve or a reply the map view is not allowed to read, looks like an outage and keeps retrying.
