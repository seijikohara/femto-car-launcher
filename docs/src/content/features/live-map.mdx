The dashboard background is a live map that fills the whole display and follows the vehicle. The cards, the clock, the speed readout and the dock float above the map. A tap on the map opens your maps app at the current position.

## Two map providers

- **OpenStreetMap (default).** OpenStreetMap data rendered through the keyless OpenFreeMap service. The provider needs no account and no application programming interface (API) key.
- **Google Maps (optional).** Roadmap, satellite, hybrid and terrain map types with a traffic overlay. The provider activates when you enter a personal Google Maps Platform API key under **Settings → Map**. Usage bills your own Google Cloud account; Femto Car Launcher adds no fees. The app stores the key in its settings and sends it only to Google. The Google Maps provider is not offered in the territories on Google's [Prohibited Territories](https://cloud.google.com/maps-platform/terms/maps-prohibited-territories) list; the default provider is unaffected. The [Terms of Service](/terms/) give the details that apply to each provider. The [install guide](/install/) explains how to set up the key, and **Settings → Map → Google Maps Platform terms** opens the terms your key is used under, including the restrictions on in-vehicle use.

## Bring your own style

The OpenStreetMap provider can load a hosted MapLibre style you name (**Settings → Appearance → Map color → Custom style URL**), including a style whose provider key is part of the URL. The map then shows that style's own credits. If you only want a mirror of the default provider's layout (a tile server closer to you, or one you run yourself), set **Settings → Map → Tile host (advanced)** instead.

## A car-navigation camera

**Settings → Map → Camera** shapes the view. **Map tilt** sets how far the camera tilts from straight down toward the horizon. **Map zoom** sets the zoom level, which the on-map zoom buttons also change. **North up** keeps north at the top of the map instead of turning it with the direction of travel. **Marker position** sets how far below the center of the map the vehicle marker sits.

- The camera follows the Global Positioning System (GPS) position with easing and keeps the direction of travel pointing up. A tap on the compass toggles a north-up mode.
- A drag detaches the camera for free panning. The camera re-attaches after a pause, or immediately when you tap the locate button.
- Zoom buttons and a compass sit at the screen edge, within reach of the driver on either side of the car.

## Route preview (nightly builds)

Nightly builds can draw a route on the OpenStreetMap provider. Tap **Show route** in the destination panel, under **Open in maps app** or on a saved place, and the launcher computes the route on the device from the current GPS position with the BRouter engine. An old position does not count as where you are: the launcher then asks the device for its current position, which can take up to about half a minute, and the panel says what is missing if none comes or while location access is off. A long press on the map opens the destination panel at the pressed point, with **Open in maps app** and **Show route** for that point; the launcher keeps the point only if you tap **Save point**. Two chips under **Show route**, **Avoid motorways** and **Avoid tolls**, set the roads a route avoids; the launcher remembers them for later routes and keeps a route's choice when it reroutes. Avoiding motorways makes a route prefer other roads and take a motorway only where no reasonable way round exists, while avoiding tolls never uses a toll road, so a destination only a toll road reaches gets no route and the panel says so. The routing data comes from brouter.de, built from OpenStreetMap, one 5° × 5° area at a time: the first route through an area shows the download size and downloads only after you confirm, and the data then stays on the device for later routes. brouter.de rebuilds the data every week, so before a route is computed, the launcher asks brouter.de whether it has rebuilt each area the route uses that it last asked about a week ago or longer. Nothing downloads for an area that has not changed. Newer data never holds a route back: the panel shows its download size, **Update** downloads it, and **Use stored data** computes the route at once with the data on the device and asks again a week later. When the route also needs an area that is not on the device yet, one confirmation covers both downloads, since the route cannot do without the missing area, and **Cancel** stops that route. The launcher checks only when you route, never in the background. **Settings → Map → Routing data** lists the areas on the device with their size and the date of their last check, and deletes one area or all of them after asking. Text you route to goes to the device's geocoder to find the place, so routing to text needs a device with one; a saved position routes without it. The map draws the line, and a row under the speed readout shows the distance left, the arrival time and a button that clears the route. When you drive off the route for a few seconds, the launcher routes again on its own from where you are to the same destination: the row says **Rerouting…**, and the new line replaces the old one. Rerouting uses only the routing data already on the device and never goes to the network, so where an area is missing, the old line stays and the row says there is no routing data. The arrival time knows no traffic. The turn card over the map, described below, shows the next turn, but the launcher speaks no directions and names no streets: **Open in maps app** still hands the destination to your navigation app. A long route can take a minute to compute or run out of time; a closer destination works better. The route lasts until you clear it, and it comes back when the app restarts unless the vehicle was parked, since the route was last driven, for longer than **Settings → Location → Start a new trip automatically** allows (2 hours by default); with **Never**, it comes back until you clear it. For that, the launcher keeps the destination, the roads to avoid, the line of the route, with its length, travel time and turns, and the time the route was last driven in files on the device, outside backup, and clearing the route deletes them. That time comes from the latest GPS fix the dashboard received while on screen with the route on. Unless the setting is **Never**, when the device's clock reads more than five minutes earlier than that time, as on a unit that boots before its clock is set, the route stays hidden until the first GPS fix tells how long the vehicle was parked. That fix also removes a route that came back on the device's clock when it shows a longer park, and drops a hidden one when it cannot tell either. A route that comes back returns alone, without other routes to choose from, and its first reroute searches from scratch, so it can take longer. Route preview stays in nightly builds until I have checked it on a head unit.

Once the first route is found, the launcher looks for up to two more routes to the same destination under the same conditions, one after the other, and a small card over the map shows each route as it is found, side by side with its travel time and length. The map draws the route found first, and the speed readout counts it down; tap another route on the card to follow it instead. While the card is open, the routes you have not chosen show as thin grey lines under the chosen one. A route along nearly the same roads as one already listed is left out, and when no other route turns up, the card says so. **Done** closes the card and keeps the route you chose, and the card also closes by itself after 30 seconds of driving. Driving off the route closes the card too, since the other routes started from where you were.

While you follow a route, a second card in the same corner of the map shows the next turn: a large arrow, the distance to the turn, and one line of text such as **Turn left** or **Take exit 2**. When the turn after it follows within about 150 m, a small second arrow shows it under **Then**. Past the last turn, the card shows a flag and the distance to the destination. Where there is too little room above the speed readout, or the space is too narrow, the card shrinks to the arrow and the distance, and keeps the small second arrow where it still fits. Where there is no room for both the card and the video window, the video window is hidden while the card shows, and its sound keeps playing. On a small display at the larger Display sizes the card can be left out altogether; the map still draws the route. The card names no roads, because the routing data has no road names. The turns come from the routing data as well, so a turn can be wrong or missing where the data is; the road and its signs come first. The card hides while the route choice card is open, while the row under the speed readout shows a reroute's status, while you are off the route, after a restart until the first GPS position places you on the route, and for a route without turn data, such as one kept by an earlier version of the launcher. Under **Guidance** in **Settings → Navigation**, **Turn card** switches the card off, and the map then shows the route alone. While the card is on, **Show the turn after next** switches the small second arrow, and **Show "Continue straight"** also shows the places where the route goes straight on across a bigger road, which the card leaves out by default.

**Settings → Navigation**, listed wherever route preview is offered, switches each of these parts on or off. **Reroute automatically** routes again when you drive off the route; off, the old line stays. **Suggest other routes** looks for the two other routes and opens the card; off, the first route is the only one. **Bring the route back after a restart** keeps the route on the device so it comes back when the app restarts; switching it off deletes the kept route at once. **Time shown for a route** picks what the row under the speed readout shows beside the distance left: the arrival time, or the time remaining as a bare duration, such as "18 min". **Keep the vehicle on the route**, under **Guidance**, draws the vehicle on the route line while its GPS position stays close to the line, and at the GPS position once it strays farther, as on a road beside the route; off, the map always draws it at the GPS position. With all of them off, the map still draws the line, and the row under the speed readout still shows the distance left and the button that clears the route.

## Depth and style

Optional three-dimensional buildings and terrain relief add depth on the OpenStreetMap provider. Map colors default to the Standard scheme, a clean palette for both the light and the dark theme. They can instead follow the launcher's accent color, or use the provider's own styles unchanged.

**Settings → Appearance → Map color** holds the color choices. **Match app theme**, on by default, gives the map the light or dark look of the launcher's theme; with it off, **Map style** sets **Light** or **Dark** for the map alone. **Light scheme** and **Dark scheme** are chosen separately. Each offers **Standard**, **Accent (app color)**, **Custom style URL** and the provider's own styles: **Positron**, **Bright** and **Liberty** for light, **Dark Matter**, **Dark** and **Fiord** for dark. Map color schemes apply to the OpenStreetMap provider only.

Under **Settings → Map**, **3D buildings** is on by default and **Terrain** is off. Terrain adds tile downloads from Mapterhorn for the elevation relief.

## Saved map regions (nightly builds)

Nightly builds can save regions of the map on the device, under **Settings → Map → Saved map regions**. The entry shows only with the OpenStreetMap provider. The map does not draw saved regions yet.

**Add region** lists the regions Geofabrik publishes, with a suggestion for the current location, and shows a region's download size before you save it. One region downloads at a time. An interrupted download pauses: **Resume** continues it, and **Cancel** discards the part already downloaded. **Storage limit** caps the total size of the saved regions; lowering it deletes nothing. Each saved region shows its size and the date of its data, and **Delete** removes it after asking.

## Automatic recovery

When the map fails to load (an unstable link, a provider outage, a missing key), a notice appears inside the visible map area instead of a blank panel. While the launcher is on screen, reloads retry with a capped backoff until the map data loads, even while the device still reports a connection. A reconnect after an offline period reloads the map at once. Behind another app the map waits: if a reload was due, the reload runs once when you return to the launcher. A request the provider answers with a refusal, such as a style address its server reports as missing or a rejected key, stops the retries after a few attempts. A failure that brings back no readable answer, such as a server name that does not resolve or a reply the map view is not allowed to read, looks like an outage and keeps retrying.
